# Prompt compilation

Load [source-lock-transcode-repair.md](source-lock-transcode-repair.md) first. Compile from its versioned source lock and compare the finished operation back to that lock before delivery.

Load [three-render-two-compilation.md](three-render-two-compilation.md) for an approved whole-operation `appearance_family: three_render_two`, or solely for an explicitly approved three-render-two layer/subject/interval within a `mixed_media` source lock. A scoped contract, not a broad style word, activates the latter. The mixed operation remains `mixed_media`; other layers keep their own visual contract and must not inherit its terminology or required dimensions. This local compilation does not require a separate generation operation.

Follow the [open appearance contract](../../visual-design/references/appearance-family-routing.md) for custom looks and mixed layers. A custom `other` contract still compiles through the exact model-native adapter; it is not permission to use an unvalidated generic API or substitute a familiar style. Preserve explicit absences and allowed variation: do not add outlines to outline-free painting, realistic specular response to flat work, smooth interpolation to approved stepping, or a default 3D camera path to planar staging. In a mixed operation, scope appearance instructions by approved layer/subject/interval. Local three-render-two clauses may share the same model-native prompt with other layers' clauses, but must name their owner and not relabel the composite. Only an independently justified, authorized production split creates a separate sub-operation. Output consistency means compliance with the permitted pattern of change, not pixel-identical texture or a universally smooth cadence.

Keep outside the pasteable prompt: model choice reasoning, upload instructions, file paths, asset approval history, continuity history, rejected alternatives and test commentary.

The prompt contains only:

- current task and reference-role mapping in platform syntax;
- source-carried state plus necessary deltas;
- the smallest sufficient appearance base-lock clauses plus the current shot appearance delta;
- visible/audible action in playback order;
- camera path and internal cuts when required;
- exact dialogue, lyrics or visible text when required by the adapter or approved picture-sync strategy;
- soundscape/music roles supported by the platform;
- required ending state and intended motion at the exit, not a compulsory freeze or settled camera;
- a short list of high-consequence exclusions.

## Sound compilation boundary

For character acting, also apply the performance section below. Audible ownership and visible performance are separate decisions.

Compile only `native_required` audible layers and the `native_optional` audible layers selected for this operation. Omit every `post_only` audible layer from soundscape/music requests, including descriptive ambience and score; keep its audible specification in the post-production handoff. Do not erase a separately approved visible mouth action, contact, exact line timing or synchronization anchor. A post-only dialogue line enters the picture operation only through its declared `silent_performance_cue`, `guide_track_not_for_master` or `external_lipsync` strategy, never as an undeclared native-audio request. `intentional_silence` may use a platform-supported silence or clean-audio control, but must not be replaced by invented ambience.

Omission is not a technical guarantee of silence. If the active platform always synthesizes audio or cannot isolate an unwanted layer, report the limitation and plan muting, source separation, a no-audio path or another control method. Do not promise that wording alone can prevent generation.

In required sound fields such as an adapter's `overall_soundscape`, describe only generation-owned audible elements. Do not paste picture cues, post handoffs or the full sound plan into those fields. Native audio is still reviewed after generation; good synchronized output may be retained and flawed output may be cleaned, supplemented or replaced.

Delete repetition before deleting requirements. Do not add generic “masterpiece/8K/cinematic” filler. Do not refer to “the previous clip” unless the actual labeled source is bound.

Do not paste a whole visual bible into every operation. Account internally for required appearance dimensions, then include only those owned by the prompt or necessary to prevent high-consequence drift.

When compiling multiple targets, compile each independently from the same lock. A target-native prompt is an output, never the source for another adapter. If the user asks only for diagnosis, do not rewrite the prompt.

Structural validation can prove field order, count, range and label correctness. It cannot prove physical plausibility, dramatic value, identity fidelity or impact.

## Model-native allocation of detail

The source lock is a design contract, not text to paste verbatim. Follow the selected adapter's manual: H3 Ref2VA explicitly develops each shot in a detailed audiovisual description; Seedance 2.5 can delegate accurately supplied motion to its reference and describe continuous action broadly with selected key moments. A sparse white model still needs missing appearance/scene information. Seedance 2.0 accepts natural-language reference/task descriptions without H3's section grammar. These differences are conditional, not fixed prompt-length rankings.

For Seedance 2.5, use [seedance-25.md](seedance-25.md) and its selected operation branch. Its primary authority is the Jimeng official manual, not the legacy third-party guide. Ordinary generation, ultra-long generation, extension and editing have different instruction scopes: a new-clip timeline is not an extension delta or an edit mask. Preserve source assignments, exact content, timing and sound ownership while changing the operation-specific structure. An unknown third-party host permits a labeled model-native draft, not a claim that labels/settings have already been applied.

Internally distinguish meaning that must survive, execution details that are exact locks, and optional explanatory detail. Carry each required item either in verified applicable reference content, prompt text or a supported control. Do not duplicate every detail across all three; do not call an item reference-covered when it is absent. Cross-model compilation starts from the shared design, then rebuilds labels, input roles, timing and sound in the target's form. If an official recommendation cannot fit the actual host, expose that conflict instead of silently dropping required meaning.

For Kling 3.0, follow [kling-3-series.md](kling-3-series.md): a supplied start frame carries the inspected initial visual state, multi-reference Omni needs explicit transfer roles, an edit needs a bounded change plus preserved motion/state, and motion control assigns movement to the actual action reference. Custom multi-shot controls and prose shot descriptions are different carriers. Do not import H3's field requirements or turn a short official editing example into a universal brevity rule; retain all material acting, spatial, timing and sound decisions.

## Semantic coverage before submission

For each material creative carrier in the source lock, identify where the operation implements it: actual reference content, prompt action/camera, a supported motion control or the declared post-production handoff. “Covered by reference” requires the inspected reference to show the relevant relationship in its approved role; “preserve composition” cannot delegate a missing composition. Text is responsible for the required change that a still cannot establish. No arbitrary prompt length or reference count is a quality target.

Compare the operation back to the intended audience-visible event. Preserve its trigger, attention target, spatial relation and consequence; remove repetition or optional decoration first. A fluent prompt containing all character names and style tags can still fail this comparison. If the plan already lacks the intended relationship, return to direction; if it was designed but disappeared in translation, repair compilation. Do not silently turn a reveal into a generic push-in, task-based acting into nodding, or a relational composition into independent talking portraits.

Where timings are explicit, reconcile ordered/overlapping actions, dialogue allowance, internal cuts and endpoint with the actual configured duration. Approximate beat times are not all additive, but conflicting hard timecodes or a required ending beyond the available clip remain unresolved until reconciled. Keep prompt-stated duration, selected setting and measured output distinct. Do not compress delivery mechanically or change exact dialogue to make an overfilled unit fit; use an authorized boundary/control change or report the conflict.

Before actual submission, use [operation-reconciliation.md](operation-reconciliation.md) to compare the approved plan and final prompt with real settings and selected reference content. Review meaning as well as numbers: repeated boilerplate must not change local hair/clothes, a locked camera must not simultaneously translate, and a reference label must not describe a different candidate from the attached one. Report missing readback instead of copying intended values into an “observed” record. A prompt-only handoff does not require opening or mutating a platform.

## Performance compilation

For a director-specific scene, preserve the approved circumstance-to-tactic-to-carrier decisions from [director-decision-practice.md](../../director/references/director-decision-practice.md). Compile observable delivery changes, body/eye coordination and event-triggered framing/time choices, not the director's name, source-film shot list or full research rationale. Keep stable voice identity separate from local delivery, exact wording unchanged, and audible ownership separate from mouth/body cues. Do not turn urgency into automatic fast cuts, high volume, slow-motion hits or a new music layer. Official model/version syntax remains authoritative; the directing guide introduces no platform controls. Text-only style comparisons are not approved generation operations.

When character acting is material, compile from the director's `performance_contract`. A bare label such as sad, happy, angry, affectionate or afraid is insufficient for a required change; it may remain as context alongside the approved visible behavior. If the contract lacks the material behavior or intensity decision, return that gap to director design rather than inventing tears, smiles, trembling or a hidden motive.

Write selected visible changes in playback order with their attention targets, triggers, relative timing, outward intensity and endpoint. Preserve an intentional hold, mixed feeling or mismatch between polite words and bodily response when specified. Do not convert everything into a static expression, a single micro-reaction, a generic gesture loop or an exaggerated climax. Do not append a catalogue of facial muscles or demand that eyes, face, breath and hands all move at once.

Before delivery, compare meaning against the source: are the required response and target present; does response follow available information; are amplitude and concealment limits preserved; is the cue visible at the chosen shot scale; does the ending carry into the next cut? Optional gestures may be omitted to reduce competition, but required acting may not disappear during compression or model transcode. Report a conflict rather than silently changing a locked camera or performance.

For precise gaze shifts, contact/wiping or other delicate details that compete with camera/action/effects, apply the existing precision-owner rule. Record the control as text-directed, reference-supported or unresolved according to actual available evidence. A supported performance reference may carry timing and motion only within its approved role; it does not authorize copying identity or story. Do not promise exact tear motion, eye tracking or emotional timing from prose alone, and do not add unverified adapter controls.

Static image operations retain the selected expression/posture/visible physical state; they cannot validate a transition, reaction timing or fluid motion. Visible breath and mouth cues continue to obey the separate picture-sync and sound-ownership contract.

## Shot, camera and time-treatment compilation (only when material)

Use the approved decisions from the director's [shot-camera-time-design.md](../../director/references/shot-camera-time-design.md), not a catalogue of cinematic terms. Preserve the visible scale/subject relationship, relevant camera side/path, focus target/transfer, reveal and intended exit. A required moving exit or interrupted move must not be normalized into a stable hold. Static images can establish framing and one instant's pose, not motion or slow playback.

Compile temporal events onto the intended generated playback timeline, distinguishing it from story time and the final edit. Slow motion, a speed ramp, a held pose, camera acceleration and output frame rate are different requests. Preserve consequential entry/exit anchors, layer ownership and synchronization; do not expand the entire unit when only one interval should slow. Add exact seconds or ratios only when required and reconcile them with actual duration/settings. Reference-carried cadence or motion must be present in the inspected reference, not inferred from its filename.

Use the exact adapter's existing grammar: H3 movement and time changes belong in the shot timeline; its cut timestamps label cuts, not every speed change. Seedance uses its own natural-language/reference conventions and version-specific guidance for timing/detail. Do not transplant H3 fields or manufacture fps, shutter, interpolation, motion-strength or speed-ramp API parameters. A desired visual effect is a text-directed target unless an actual supported control/reference establishes more; a passed structure check is not precision validation.

If the director assigns retiming to post, generate suitable source action and required handles without also asking the model to apply the same slowdown, unless the two-stage treatment was deliberately planned. Keep approved native/picture-sync audio relationships intact and post-only sound outside the audible request. Expose an unsupported hard timing/focus/camera lock rather than silently approximating it or changing the selected style.

## Action and scale compilation (only when material)

For multi-opponent action, preserve who currently draws attention, the active threat, consequential actions/positions of other participants, why focus changes, and the new state. Convert planning labels into visible action and framing in the target's supported form; do not feed internal taxonomies as if the model knew them. Use approved visual/spatial anchors to disambiguate people without adding costume changes or weapons.

For powers, preserve the established trigger, effect ownership, target and observable consequence. For scale-critical imagery, preserve the selected comparisons, support/occlusion, reveal and authorized environment responses. Prefer a concrete relation over extra giant/epic/8K adjectives. Do not import a 3D look, a scale figure, mist, rubble or native sound that the source did not approve.

Compare the compiled result semantically to those decisions: did compression erase an incoming threat, turn intentional waiting into idle extras, change simultaneous action into a queue, remove the sole size comparison or let VFX replace the actual cause? An approved offscreen threat may remain offscreen; every participant need not be named in every shot. Never impose a universal cast cap, cut count or seconds per exchange.

If overlapping contacts, identities or scale relationships exceed evidenced control, expose the conflict and propose coverage, reference/control changes or a unit boundary preserving the approved beat. Do not silently reduce opponents, change the outcome or weaken the acceptance standard. A passing syntax/structure check cannot prove readable action, mass or model execution.
