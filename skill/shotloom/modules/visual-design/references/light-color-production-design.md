# Light, color and production design as story decisions

Use when attention, atmosphere, evolving light/color or environment/prop meaning needs design. Apply inside the approved appearance family; this is not a realism/PBR requirement or a mandatory color script for a local adjustment.

## Light and color through the scene

Start with what needs to be perceived and what may remain obscure. Relate subject/background value, color, edge and texture contrast to that hierarchy at the intended display size. Saturation, darkness, shallow focus or rim light do not automatically create drama. Preserve readable performance/contact when required; an intentionally obscured event needs other authorized evidence, not accidental underexposure.

Define consequential source direction, extent/softness, enclosure, time/weather and motivated changes only as applicable. When a subject or camera moves, decide which relationships persist and which surfaces enter or leave light. Do not paint identical front light onto every angle regardless of established space. Graphic 2D, expressive cel shading or surreal lighting may deliberately depart from physical simulation; make that rule coherent instead of imposing photoreal falloff.

Across a sequence, choose a baseline and meaningful color/value departures tied to place, time, viewpoint or dramatic development. A palette shift has no universal emotional meaning. Compare neighboring shots and repeated locations; do not apply the same fashionable grade to every scene or use grading to fix missing source separation. Approved controlled sublooks retain entry/exit and identity anchors.

## Environments, costume and props

Read space as usable geography and evidence of the authorized lives/world: access, wear, maintenance, density, scale, object arrangement and material contrast. Choose details supporting action, relation or context; do not invent backstory, wealth, allegiances, destruction or plot clues as decoration. Keep meaningful negative space as deliberately as detail.

For a recurring object/image, separate physical continuity from dramatic use: who handles or notices it, when it is revealed/withheld, and whether recurrence changes meaning. A prop does not need a close-up every time it appears. Costume and set surfaces should preserve silhouette, movement/contact and attention hierarchy, not only attractiveness in a standalone asset portrait.

Pass stable construction/geometry to the asset package, scene light/color deltas to the visual bible and perceptual intent back to director/review. If a design cannot carry the shot's function, revise the scoped composition/material/light relationship; do not silently redesign an approved identity or location. Validate in representative contexts using existing evidence or separately authorized tests; one beauty still does not prove all angles or motion.
