# Relative scale and perceived mass

Use only when size, distance or mass materially affects the requested design: a giant creature, monumental structure, enormous weapon, vehicle/interior relationship or deliberately toy-like world. Keep the approved appearance family and story geography. This guide describes visible relationships, not a rendering pipeline.

## Smallest sufficient scale design

Choose the cues that carry the intended reading; do not require all of them or a fixed number:

- **Comparison:** use an existing character, doorway, vehicle, facade or terrain feature whose size is legible. Describe the relevant relation, such as clearance, occupied width or reach across a known space. A numerical height alone rarely communicates the whole relationship, but exact dimensions remain locked when supplied.
- **Depth and overlap:** make clear which surfaces hide which, where the subject meets its support and how it relates to known anchors. Partial reveal can suggest extent; a full-body or centered view can be correct when establishing anatomy, geography or a deliberate graphic composition.
- **Viewpoint and reveal:** choose a camera relationship that allows the comparison to register. A low angle is one option, not a default. Do not crop out the only scale cue for spectacle or invent a camera move against a locked composition.
- **Environment response:** when authorized and physically or stylistically appropriate, use support loading, displaced water, moving loose material, shadow coverage or existing atmospheric layers. Tie the response to the subject's actual action and time of arrival. Destruction, weather, bystanders and sound are not automatic additions.
- **Movement and mass:** specify support, acceleration, directional change, settling and material response only where they matter. Large does not always mean slow; designed agility may be part of the approved creature or vehicle. Mass should not be reduced to slow motion, perpetual camera shake or obligatory deep sound.

Keep dramatic intent explicit: awe, vulnerability, intimacy, absurdity, wonder and dread may need different reveal and pacing. Not every giant scene needs aggression or damage.

## Continuity and handoff

If multiple views depend on the same scale, preserve the relation to stable anchors across cuts and distinguish lens/framing changes from an actual size change. Do not resize a subject to fit a new frame or shrink an environment to make it look imposing.

For a scale-critical shot, pass only its selected comparison anchors, important relative dimensions, depth/support relationships, chosen reveal, permitted environment responses and forbidden drift. Mark what an approved reference carries versus what needs text or another control. Use the existing visual contract/source-lock representation; no new compulsory package is required for ordinary shots.

When adding a scale cue would introduce a new story fact or alter approved geography, explain the gap and request the appropriate creative decision. Prefer available anchors before proposing new assets. Do not independently assign native sound: audible ownership remains in the director/sound contract.

## Appearance and review limits

Hand-drawn 2D may communicate size through graphic proportion, overlap and staged poses without realistic lens depth, volumetric fog or 3D parallax. Standard 3D, cel-shaded, photographic, stop-motion and mixed-media work retain their own material and motion rules. Small stylized objects can intentionally resemble miniatures.

At review, compare the actual size reading to the intended anchors, not the number of scale adjectives in the prompt. A still can establish a proportion or overlap, not perceived weight over time. For motion, inspect normal playback and relevant frames for sliding support, changing proportions, inconsistent occlusion, disconnected environment response or effects that accidentally make the subject look miniature. Route unexplained drift as a finding; do not reject intentional flatness, centered framing, stillness or approved stylized physics.
