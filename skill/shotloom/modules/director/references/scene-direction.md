# Scene direction

## Scene contract

For a causal dramatic scene, record entering facts and audience knowledge, each character's immediate objective, the concrete trigger, dominant conflict, turn, consequential ending state and the next bridge. If nothing changes, check whether audience understanding, tension, relationship or an intentional hold develops; if none does, strengthen, shorten or remove it. Do not invent an irreversible story event merely to fill a form.

For montage, atmosphere or an intentional pause within the drama, define its sequence-level purpose, organizing relation, development and exit. Individual images may build place, sensory experience, mood, rhythm, elapsed time, motif or contrast without each changing a character's decision. Mark discontinuities intentionally; use shared action/state only where continuity is actually intended. This branch does not authorize converting a scene into an unrelated MV or trailer.

## From intention to a perceptible design

For a selected director method, connect circumstance and character tactic to audience access, a primary carrier and the event that changes or sustains it. Use [director-decision-practice.md](director-decision-practice.md) when this translation or its alternatives need development. Do not choose cuts, close-ups or voice intensity directly from an emotion label. Keep the source-supported method separate from this scene's authored choices; reuse the contract rather than adding a new form.

For an important beat, identify what the audience should notice and what actually carries it: a changing distance, an interrupted task, a withheld view, a prop interaction, a sound-picture relation, a held composition or another concrete choice. This is the **creative carrier** of the beat, not a new plot fact or a mandatory spectacular moment. Reuse the existing performance/camera/sound contract; add only the missing relationship, not another complete form.

Test the design against a plain alternative. Would replacing it with interchangeable faces delivering the same lines preserve everything the audience needs? If yes, determine whether plain coverage is intentionally sufficient or whether direction has left the intended effect unspecified. If the choice is still genuinely open, briefly compare materially different staging/coverage approaches within the approved method and select for audience effect and feasibility. Do not generate alternatives or expand the story merely to perform this comparison.

Name the required relationship and its visible minimum separately from optional embellishment. For example, when a character's task is interrupted by an arrival, the task, access to the arrival and resulting attention shift can carry the scene; neither a dramatic facial close-up nor a moving camera is automatically necessary. If restrained behavior is chosen, specify what remains readable rather than successively suppressing gesture, voice and gaze until nothing communicates. Expressive, stylized or comic performance remains valid under its approved scale.

Pass the carrier, visibility condition and forbidden substitution through existing `must_preserve` and review decisions. For instance, preserving a refusal is not the same as preserving the particular finger motion used to express it, unless that motion is an exact lock. A designed stillness may pass; a missing required response cannot be renamed restraint after generation. Sound-dependent carriers retain their separate audible ownership.

## Blocking

Translate power and attention into distance, level, orientation, access and obstruction. Track who approaches, yields, blocks an exit, owns the center, controls a prop and sees what. Purposeful movement may change or sustain a task, spatial relation, attention or power; not every incidental movement must create a dramatic turn.

Do not use “walks naturally” as a complete instruction. State start position, path, obstacle, destination, eyeline and resulting power relation.

## Coverage

- Establish geography before spatially complex action.
- Use reaction shots for a readable response, sustained attention or meaningful withholding; do not add empty reaction coverage by habit.
- Use inserts for a named function: causal evidence, tactile detail, atmosphere, rhythm, time compression, motif, transition or emotional/relational information. Test their contribution in sequence; attractive detail alone does not justify endless coverage.
- Use a moving master when motion clarifies space, reveals information, shifts attention or reassigns power.
- Use a locked frame when pressure comes from withholding movement.

Shot-size change is a design choice, not a generation boundary. A continuous event may include internal cuts if each composition is stated absolutely and the execution platform can carry the shared state.

For a montage, state why these images belong together and how contrast, recurrence or compression progresses; avoid a succession of interchangeable beauty shots. For example, a cooling untouched cup can sustain a wait without changing a decision, while repeated unrelated cup close-ups may add nothing. Judge the total duration by the intended audience effect, not the number of panels or a preset seconds-per-image rule.

## Sequence-level coverage check

When designing or diagnosing a scene/sequence, read adjacent compositions together. Track whose experience organizes each phase, what is withheld/revealed, where the important relationship is visible, and what motivates a hold or cut. Similar medium shots, frontal blocking and equal turn-taking can be correct, but must not become the accidental default merely because each generation is easy. A new speaker alone does not dictate a cut; a reaction, ongoing task or changing access to information may own the picture.

Do not solve sameness with a quota of close-ups, mandatory camera motion or unrelated inserts. Variation must develop the scene's intended pressure, sensation, relationship or information; repetition can deliberately sustain it. Keep continuous spatial anchors even when the attention owner changes. Design only coverage that has a function; no requirement to produce every angle or a grid for every beat.

Before handing a material beat downstream, check whether the chosen shot makes its carrier readable and whether the available reference/control plan can support it. Resolve missing staging or framing upstream rather than relying on a generic identity board plus “reframe cinematically.” A cost/control limitation should produce an explicit tradeoff within authorization, not a silently lower creative target.

## Organize the scene's viewing relationship

Choose the initial relation between viewer and event, then the consequential change: shared space becoming isolation, restricted access becoming revelation, an observer becoming implicated, or a deliberately unchanging view through shifting power. These are options derived from the scene, not prescribed arcs. Map only meaningful phases, with the perceptual reason for holding or changing scale/viewpoint.

Decide when two people must coexist in one frame, when separation changes their relationship, and whose listening/action should own the image while another speaks. A close-up gains meaning from context and withheld access, not from closeness alone. If a held master already carries the change, additional coverage may weaken it; if it hides the needed response, plan the specific complementary view.

Compare successive shots for information gained/lost, intimacy/distance, motion energy, duration and sound perspective. Avoid identical scale and turn-taking by accident, but do not impose variation when repetition creates pressure or comedy. Preview the scene as a sequence before concluding that individually attractive frames form effective direction.

## Causal chain

```text
geography -> preparation -> initiation -> trajectory -> contact/change -> consequence -> recovery/new state
```

Keep the established causal relationship readable across the coverage. When the intended beat requires seeing contact, do not hide absent or incorrect contact behind an effect. Deliberate offscreen action, ellipsis or a reaction cut may imply an event when that serves the approved scene; sound and aftermath must remain consistent with it. Non-contact powers follow their approved trigger rather than invented contact. Do not reset bodies, damage, props or emotion after the consequence.
