# Performance and camera

## Performance beat

```text
entering state + objective -> perceived trigger -> response or deliberate withholding -> resulting state
```

This is a causal guide, not a compulsory sequence of poses. A beat may sustain, intensify, recede, mix, conceal or redirect an emotion; it need not contain suppression, a visible leak or recovery. For emotional and bodily performance, use [whole-body-performance.md](whole-body-performance.md). Select observable changes that serve this character and situation; do not schedule every facial muscle or animate every body part.

Voice direction uses loudness, timbre/tension, pace and phrase grouping, pause placement, and pitch/ending contour. Preserve exact approved dialogue.

## Camera contract

For framing, viewpoint, movement, focus and time-treatment selection, use [shot-camera-time-design.md](shot-camera-time-design.md). Specify the decisions that materially determine the shot: purpose, readable starting composition, relevant spatial anchors, movement/subject relationship, attention transfer and intended exit. Add height, side, speed, amplitude or focus timing when their difference changes the result; do not turn every simple shot into a compulsory parameter inventory.

Describe changes of mechanism in ordered phases. Simultaneous mechanisms are valid when they cooperate in one intelligible path; do not stack orbit, roll, crane, zoom and subject tracking as unrelated decoration.

Resolve physical contradictions before compilation: a locked camera position can change focal length, but a conventional dolly zoom requires camera translation with compensating focal-length change. Do not specify both an immobile position and a dolly zoom for the same phase. If a digital/graphic simulation is intended, name that separate method and intended visible result without pretending it is a physical move. Preserve an intentional compound move when its mechanisms cooperate, rather than banning all combinations.

When depth or a reveal matters, state the fixed spatial anchors, moving subject/layer and occlusion or parallax relationship. Do not add foreground clutter just to make a frame “cinematic”; purposeful flatness and unobstructed holds remain valid.

## Action camera

For a dense action phase, make the relevant action vector and camera relationship readable; leading, paralleling or trailing are choices, not mandatory movement. Preserve the geography needed to understand the beat. For contact-emphasis shake, tie the jolt to the actual event and specify its useful decay or cut exit. Handheld following, vehicle vibration, environmental disturbance and subjective instability have different causes and may occur before contact; do not manufacture an impact to justify them.

Do not bind the camera to every fist or weapon tip. If camera, body and VFX all travel with identical speed and easing, impact becomes smooth but weak.
