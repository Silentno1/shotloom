# Stylized action, impact and spectacle

Read this file for combat, chase, powers, transformations, anime PVs and other designed action.

## Combat narrative contract

Lock why the conflict happens now, each side's objective and available options, initial advantage, vulnerabilities, meaningful changes of leverage and required endpoint. A reversal or irreversible loss is required only when the story calls for it; retreat, interruption, stalemate and unresolved threat are valid endpoints. A sequence of attractive attacks without state progression is not a dramatic fight.

## Character combat language

For each fighter define preferred distance; center of gravity and stance; tempo; defense; weapon/power ownership; signature preparation, contact, color/material and sound; injury compensation; and features forbidden from migrating to another character.

## Impact levels

- L0: locomotion, feint or setup.
- L1: normal exchange or pressure.
- L2: effective hit with visible consequence.
- L3: heavy hit, reversal or major spatial change.
- L4: signature move, finisher or irreversible spectacle.

Do not make every beat L3/L4. Contrast creates force.

## Five impact layers

1. **Body mechanics:** support, hips/torso transmission, acceleration, contact, recoil, balance loss and recovery.
2. **Time shaping:** preparation, rapid acceleration, very brief contact emphasis, consequence and purposeful aftermath. No fixed seconds or frames.
3. **Deformation/limited animation:** pose hold, anticipation compression, smear, silhouette stretch, impact drawing, displaced clothing/hair and persistent damage according to the medium.
4. **Camera/edit:** a motivated scale change, cut, directional follow, bounded jolt, framing break or held view as appropriate; a beat need not use exactly one change or any camera movement.
5. **VFX/sound:** one primary graphic event and material-specific transient that reveal contact and consequence rather than mask them.

Each beat assigns one primary spectacle owner. Other layers reinforce it.

For shot scale, camera choice, slow motion, speed ramps or holds, use [shot-camera-time-design.md](shot-camera-time-design.md). Distinguish a brief impact accent from extending the entire exchange; neither slow motion nor a moving camera is compulsory. Carry the chosen temporal scope and picture/sound relationships, not a fixed duration recipe.

## Multi-opponent choreography (only when relevant)

Plan the fight as changing relationships, not several copies of a duel. Keep the smallest sufficient beat map: current attention anchor and threat, others' consequential positions/actions, cause of any focus switch, resulting advantage and carried state. This is director planning, not a mandatory platform field or a new document for every shot.

- Establish where threats, allies, obstacles and exits are relative to one another. Track the current line of action; when it changes, make the turn, relocation, threat entry or spatial reorientation readable. Crossing an axis is a motivated design choice, not a universal prohibition.
- Give each relevant participant a reason for what they are doing while someone else acts: approaching, covering an exit, recovering, hesitating, protecting, searching for an opening or being obstructed. Deliberate stillness can express fear, discipline or tactical waiting. Reject unexplained turn-taking, not every pause.
- Select a readable focus per dense beat. Foreground obstruction, eyelines, focus, silhouette and sound may signal another threat without giving everyone equal emphasis. An ensemble may deliberately transfer attention to a second pair or offscreen threat; the protagonist need not appear in every shot.
- A change of opponent should follow an available cue, choice or physical event, not a teleport. Preserve injuries, weapons, balance, locations and recovery across the change.
- Use approved distinctions in silhouette, costume, stance or equipment to keep roles legible. If uniforms are locked, use existing spatial/action differences rather than silently redesigning characters or adding weapons.
- Simultaneous attacks, assists and collisions are allowed when their ownership and consequences remain readable. Do not impose one-attacker-at-a-time or a universal headcount limit. If execution fails, isolate the evidenced overlap or change coverage/control; do not automatically remove opponents or weaken the threat.
- A fighter may escape, stall, protect someone or lose. Do not force every opponent to receive a hit, a fixed acceleration pattern, a hero victory or an obligatory finisher.

## Powers and large-scale action (only when relevant)

For an approved power, formation or large weapon, carry the established activation condition, origin, travel or area of influence, target, counterplay/defense, cost or limit, and resulting state wherever material. A non-contact effect follows its established trigger; do not invent physical contact or a new power rule to fit a template.

Scale spectacle through changes of reach, exposure, visibility, access or terrain that matter to the beat. Show an opponent responding to available information; a larger flash alone is not escalation. Assign the important event a readable preparation, peak and aftermath without fixed rounds, seconds, effect size or camera counts. Quiet dread, failed activation and interrupted attacks remain valid.

When extraordinary physical scale itself needs design, use the visual skill's [scale-and-mass.md](../../visual-design/references/scale-and-mass.md). Visual scale design does not authorize new destruction, casualties, weather or geography.

## Chase, escape and pursuit

Define pursuit/escape objectives, relative positions, available routes, line of sight, obstacles and the condition for gaining/losing contact. Build beats from consequential choices: an obstacle changes distance, a shortcut costs visibility, a turn changes who can intercept. Do not add these events without story authority. Speed lines and fast camera motion alone do not demonstrate gaining ground.

Choose coverage that preserves the needed geography or intentionally restricts knowledge. Re-establish distance/route when a turn, elevation or cut changes the relation; keep screen direction useful without forbidding motivated reorientation. Vary effort, traction, momentum and recovery by body/vehicle and approved medium. Judge acceleration against fixed anchors, not an equally sliding background. The endpoint may be escape, capture, loss of sight or unresolved pursuit; no mandatory collision or finisher.

## Transformation and PV organization

For an authorized transformation, preserve identity anchors and define what changes, its causal trigger, materially distinct phases, ownership of effects and final functional state. Track costume/props/body support through occlusion; a flash cannot excuse an unintended new face or missing object. Metamorphosis may be gradual, abrupt, graphic or partly offscreen according to the work, not a compulsory slow reveal. Review readable transition and endpoint in motion, not just two appealing stills.

For a PV, choose an organizing promise, contrast or progression from the approved work and the sound plan. Build setup, variation, accent and exit as appropriate rather than concatenating maximum-intensity shots. Nonlinear excerpts need declared time/space relations; do not claim they form continuous combat. A PV's compression is not automatically a suitable episode rhythm.

## Anti-smoothness audit

Flag unintentional loss of the approved rhythm or causality: camera, body and VFX collapsing into one eased speed curve; interchangeable trails; an impact flash falsely signaling a hit before contact or hiding contact that must be readable; unsupported limb motion; opponents resetting after each exchange; damage/position disappearing; or camera drift erasing an intended hold or reversal. Intentional charging light, feints, smooth motion and non-contact powers are not failures by themselves. Judge effect prominence against the beat's intended emphasis, not an obligatory largest-effect-at-climax formula.

## Generation-unit implication

One generation may contain several exchanges and internal cuts when identity, geography and state remain compatible. Split only for incompatible references, exact keyframe needs, cross-space/media changes, repeatedly failed critical contact, a platform limit or an intentional hidden join.

The final edit may use fractions of a generated clip. Never stretch one blow to fill the model's allowed duration.

## Review

Review dense frames around preparation, contact and consequence. Check silhouette, support foot, hip/torso transmission, contact ownership, reaction delay, camera/VFX timing, damage memory and endpoint—not only whether the clip looks energetic.
