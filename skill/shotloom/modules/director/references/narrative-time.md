# Narrative time and audience knowledge

Use when chronology, parallel action, dreams, memory, unreliable accounts, repetition or ellipsis materially changes the scene. For local playback effects use [shot-camera-time-design.md](shot-camera-time-design.md); slow motion is not a substitute for narrative-time design.

Keep separate: what happens in the story; when and whether it is established as fact; who knows it; what the audience has seen or inferred; and the order/duration of presentation. A character's belief is not objective canon. A flashback can be factual, subjective or uncertain; its label alone does not determine truth. Do not resolve authorized ambiguity by inventing a hidden truth.

Use stable event identifiers where a complex sequence needs them. A compact event map can name story-time/context, fact status, character access, audience reveal point and carried state. Do not require this map for a straightforward local scene. Review owns its accepted evidence/state implementation through [narrative contexts](../../review-continuity/references/narrative-contexts.md).

## Choosing temporal organization

- **Parallel action:** establish whether events truly overlap, merely rhyme or are deliberately misleading. Anchor the consequential shared clock (a departure, arrival, countdown, signal or other approved event) and what each side can know. Crosscut at a changing possibility, withheld answer or contrast, not automatically at equal intervals. Travel/action time and audio bridges must not accidentally imply an impossible meeting.
- **Ellipsis:** choose what the viewer may infer, the before/after evidence needed and which uncertainty remains intentional. Omission can compress repetition or sharpen a reveal; it does not automatically undo the omitted story event. If the edit loses the only evidence needed to understand the outcome, restore evidence or seek a changed narrative decision.
- **Delay and anticipation:** separate audience knowledge from character knowledge to choose suspense, surprise or dramatic irony. Do not add a reaction to information the character has not received. Longer is not automatically more tense; hold only while expectation develops or purposeful discomfort remains.
- **Return/repetition:** a repeated event should change context, access, interpretation or felt rhythm when that is its purpose. It need not introduce a new fact. Mark the return point and any altered viewpoint; do not propagate an old injury/prop/wardrobe state into the present merely because it appears later in the edit.
- **Memory/dream/account:** define entry/exit signals only as needed for the desired clarity or ambiguity. They may be performance, sound, composition or context rather than a universal dissolve/color filter. Approved sublooks remain governed by visual design. A subjective image is not evidence that its contents happened objectively.

Rehearse the presentation with temporary sound. Ask what the viewer believes at each consequential boundary and whether that matches the intended experience. Clear chronology is not always the goal; deliberate uncertainty still needs controlled information and a coherent authorized basis.
