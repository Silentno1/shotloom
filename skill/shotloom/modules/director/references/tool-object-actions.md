# Tool-object actions

For cutting, peeling, sawing, scraping, drilling or similar work, define support, operating hand, actual working edge/point, contact geometry, one primary feed direction, material response after the working part passes, and final state of tool, hands, object and residue.

Never infer the cutting direction from a tool tip or screen-left/right alone. Identify the working edge and local material relation first, then translate to screen direction. For rotational feed, define the object's axis, primary driver and actual stabilizing support when relevant; do not invent a second hand if a fixture, surface or one-handed method supplies support.

Choose coverage that makes the required support, working edge, contact and result understandable. A wide demonstration may show both hands together; an insert or intentional offscreen action may distribute that information across shots without inventing support or hiding a required contact failure. Do not require both hands in every frame. If the first frame's contact geometry is impossible, repair the frame; motion prose cannot reliably fix it.
