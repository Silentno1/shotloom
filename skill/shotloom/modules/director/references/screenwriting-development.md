# Screenwriting development

Use structure as diagnosis, never as a compulsory beat sheet. For a whole-work diagnosis or method, read the full authoritative work and identify protagonist drive, opposition, stakes, knowledge, choices, consequences, escalation, reversals, climax and ending state. For a local scene/episode question, read that scope and the immediately relevant established setup/state; do not require the entire work or issue whole-work conclusions from the excerpt.

Formal screenplay pages contain only visible or audible events. Director notes may separately record intention and subtext. For an authorized story-fact change, identify affected authoritative versions and derived state, then reconcile within the approved write scope; disclose out-of-scope dependencies rather than silently editing them. A diagnosis or proposal does not change canon.

For short-form episodes, choose an opening that earns attention through an immediate question, pressure, relationship, image or purposeful atmosphere without falsifying the episode. Diagnose progression in knowledge, relationship, position, resource, risk, commitment or audience understanding. For intentional pauses or montage, use the sequence-level purpose in [scene-direction.md](scene-direction.md), not an obligatory reversal in every image. Action spectacle should contribute to the dramatic progression rather than interrupt it.

## Conflict progression and emotional promise

For a requested scene/episode diagnosis, identify what the audience is being invited to anticipate or feel and what makes that expectation change. This is a diagnostic lens, not a required humiliation/revenge arc, numeric emotion curve or fixed beat sheet.

When pressure and response recur, compare what is different after each exchange: new evidence, a changed tactic, an exposed priority, a resource spent, a narrowed option, a revealed relationship or a changed interpretation. Check the listener's available information and response as well as the speaker's line. Repeated words can earn their place through changed meaning, performance or audience knowledge; repetition without a new function should be challenged, not merely intensified with louder dialogue.

Use a compact before -> intervention/response -> after comparison only where needed to locate a stagnant passage. Do not invent an extra incident, insult, injury or disclosure to satisfy the comparison. Suggest director-level emphasis when the source already contains the change; obtain approval when the story itself needs rewriting.

For longer episodes, look for sustained pressure, discoveries, relief and renewed uncertainty where the story needs them. Do not prescribe a midpoint hook by the clock, equal-length rounds, a cliffhanger on every episode or a universal dialogue-character limit. A resolved ending can fulfill the intended episode function. Judge spoken duration with the intended delivery and available timing evidence, not script character count alone.

## Setup, payoff and knowledge continuity

When a multi-scene/episode task requires it, compare relevant setups and payoffs against existing authoritative records: where a promise/question was planted, who knows what, present status, constraints on a payoff and any resolution already written. Include relationship, resource, prop or rule changes only when consequential. Reuse the existing ledger; do not create a second canon or mark an intended payoff as achieved in generated media.

Distinguish deliberate withholding or recurrence from forgotten setup, unsupported revelation and repeated exposition. An unresolved thread is not automatically a defect or an instruction to pay it off immediately. Keep observations and proposed revisions separate from authorized changes; no ledger writes are implied by diagnosis.
