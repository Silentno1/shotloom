# Director editing from actual media

## Previsualization before actual takes

For authorized previs, assemble identified boards, spatial mock-ups or existing rough assets with provisional durations and representative temporary sound. Keep board IDs and planned time separate from actual-media timecodes. Test information flow, scene geography, exact dialogue space, overlap, holds, reveals and transitions at playback speed; revise responsible directing/design choices before costly dependent work. Static boards can test composition/order/timing but cannot validate generated acting, contact or motion.

Use only the detail needed to settle the uncertainty. A simple shot with adequate evidence need not get a separate animatic. Preserve the approved story/method; temporary assets are not automatically approved production references or cleared release material. Previs creation does not authorize paid generation or final picture lock.

## Three layers

1. **Technical usable range:** no blocking corruption, missing dialogue, broken contact or unrepairable artifact.
2. **Dramatic usable range:** the portion that carries the intended trigger, reaction, turn, impact or endpoint.
3. **Sequence fit:** the portion that works with neighboring shots, rhythm, geography and sound.

Record source hash/path, actual duration, source frame rate, timecode convention, picture in/out, audio in/out, handles, cut reason, bridge, continuity note and excluded ranges.

For a material rescue crop, replacement, trim or retime, also name the original dramatic function/creative carrier and check what the repair removes. Hiding an erroneous counter can remove the shared space that made a negotiation readable; retaining correct dialogue alone does not settle that tradeoff. Record the result as preserved, altered but still meeting the approved intent, lost or unverified. This belongs in the existing edit note, not another ledger. Repair does not grant permission to alter a creative lock; an accepted local compromise is not the future visual baseline.

If the needed relationship is available in other approved material, select coverage that preserves it within edit authorization. Otherwise disclose the missing function and the scoped options/cost before requesting regeneration or lowering the standard. Do not progressively crop every problem into close talking faces merely to make all generated clips usable.

For every material native sound, record one treatment: `retain`, `clean`, `supplement`, `replace` or `intentional_silence`, with the reason. Preserve synchronized native dialogue, impacts and prop contact when they sound natural; do not rebuild them merely for consistency with a preferred workflow. Add or replace only what is missing, wrong, damaged or unable to bridge neighboring material.

For adjacent shots in one location, compare scene-level acoustic identity, source density and perspective. Do not force identical volume across changes in camera distance, enclosure, doors, attention or point of view. When an established ambience or crowd layer disappears without a motivated change, bridge it with approved native material or post-production sound. Do not add ambience to a scene whose intended design is quiet.

Before picture lock, build a representative temporary soundtrack for any dialogue, ambience, effect, cue or music layer that can change cut timing or audience comprehension. Temporary means replaceable, not optional: identify its ownership, sync anchors and intended final source. It need not have final cleanup, design or loudness, but it must be credible enough to judge the edit. Do not lock picture against silence and discover later that the approved post-only sound changes the rhythm.

For action, cut around readable preparation, peak and consequence—not simply at every strike. Compress repeated attacks or dead travel; preserve state-changing impact and aftermath. Smooth source motion may require trimming or selective retiming, but retiming must not create impossible contact or audio drift.

## Retiming and motion at the cut

For an approved slow/fast passage, speed ramp or freeze, retain the director's [time-treatment intent](../../director/references/shot-camera-time-design.md). Record source and timeline ranges separately, meaningful transition anchors and speed mapping; distinguish source interpretation, frame duplication/blending, interpolation and a change already synthesized in the clip. Do not apply a second slowdown merely because the source carries a slow-motion label. For a constant-speed interval, timeline duration equals source duration divided by playback-speed multiplier; a ramp requires its actual varying mapping rather than averaging endpoint speeds.

Inspect actual source cadence, repeated frames, motion blur, occlusions and contact before choosing a retime method. Output fps alone does not establish unique high-rate motion samples, and optical-flow interpolation cannot be assumed artifact-free. Preserve intentional stepped animation and pose holds unless a cadence change was approved. Recheck contact, gaze, deforming silhouettes, in/out state and neighboring rhythm after processing; do not present a retimed preview as accepted source canon.

Handle audio separately under the existing ownership plan. Preserve exact words, lip timing and any locked music/event sync; decide explicitly which native layers are retained, retimed, detached, supplemented or replaced. A picture-speed change does not authorize stretching every sound or changing dialogue. Preserve continued movement across an intentional cut; do not force a settled ending onto every source clip. Unsupported source detail or insufficient handles require a scoped tradeoff, not invented missing action.

If the changed cut carries captions, [subtitle-conform.md](subtitle-conform.md) maps them through the actual audible source selection, including J/L cuts and repeated source uses, rather than copying picture offsets. Verify material sound anchors in the actual export with [output-sync-calibration.md](output-sync-calibration.md); neither source timestamps nor a correct timeline placement proves rendered synchronization.

## Montage, atmosphere and inserts

Judge an insert by its sequence function, not only by whether an object changes a decision. Place, sensation, waiting, motif, time compression and contrast can be valid. Evaluate the buildup, recurrence/variation, sound relationship and exit at normal playback; remove redundant beauty shots that add no intended effect. A held image need not contain constant motion or a plot reversal.

In a declared montage, distinguish intentional time/space jumps from accidental continuity breaks. Do not demand matching action across deliberately discontinuous images, or excuse broken causal contact in a continuous event as “montage.” Do not fill the edit with every storyboard panel or preserve a clip's full generated duration. Representative temporary sound still matters when the rhythm depends on music or ambience; montage is not permission to generate post-only layers natively.

## Rough-cut review

Preserve the approved circumstance-to-audience decision under [director-decision-practice.md](../../director/references/director-decision-practice.md): distinguish acting speed, speech, within-frame motion, cut frequency, time treatment and music pulse. Choose an event/attention change or meaningful continuation, not an automatic cut per speaker or per emotion. Judge voice/body/listening together and retain needed reactions and carryover; a faster cut may erase the selected method's effect. Reference-film durations are not an edit template. Resolve absent material explicitly rather than silently replacing the chosen method or initiating paid comparisons.

Review at the scope of the requested cut, not only shot by shot. Distinguish assembly (is the necessary material present?), structure (does order/access deliver the intended story?), performance (do selected responses and relationships develop?), and rhythm (do duration, movement, quiet and sound create the intended experience?). These are judgment passes, not mandatory separate renders or sequential approvals for each small edit.

For an episode, compare its major phases and returns: which question/pressure engages the viewer, what develops, where attention or emotional energy unintentionally drops, and whether the ending follows or deliberately reframes what preceded it. A series of technically clean takes may still be monotonous; diagnose missing contrasts, withheld information, redundant exposition, weak reaction selection or uniform spectacle before proposing more generations. Preserve purposeful flatness, ambiguity or repetition when they actually serve the work.

State the observed viewer-facing effect alongside compliance with the plan. A plan can be faithfully executed and still need an authorized creative revision; do not rename an ineffective result successful because it passed a checklist. Equally, a different optional gesture can work better without violating any lock. Ground judgments in actual ranges and intended audience experience, not universal cut-rate or duration scores.

Before lock, reconcile changed selections/events using [narrative-contexts.md](../../review-continuity/references/narrative-contexts.md). Technical selection is not authority to remove a story event, and an omitted depiction may still be a valid ellipsis.

Review the sequence again after technical salvage. Compare the actual progression of attention, relationships and tension/sensation with director intent, including deliberate repetition or quiet holds. A series of individually usable clips can still flatten every beat into the same visual pattern. Cut for the selected event and its consequence, not by generated duration or sentence boundaries alone; do not shorten a meaningful pause just to increase shot count. Required post-only sound must be represented before judging a sound-dependent beat.

- Normal viewing: story, attention, emotion and pace.
- Silent viewing: geography, performance, action and visual rhythm.
- Audio-only: dialogue clarity, room perspective, effects hierarchy, music logic and bridges.

Picture lock requires all three to pass or an explicit accepted exception.
